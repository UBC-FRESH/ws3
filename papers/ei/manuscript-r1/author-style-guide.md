# Author Style Guide (derived from 6 writing samples)

## Voice and stance
- Formal, analytical, and sober; avoids promotional language or hype.
- Uses first‑person plural (“we”) for methodological actions, choices, and structure.
- Establishes credibility through precise definitions and concrete statements, not appeals.
- Acknowledges limits with measured, factual language (e.g., “may,” “cannot expect,” “in practice”).

## Sentence and paragraph structure
- Predominantly declarative sentences, often multi‑clause; average 22–28 words.
- Dense paragraphs that carry a full idea (problem → rationale → consequence).
- Frequent use of parenthetical clarifications (i.e., e.g.) and hyphenated modifiers.
- Transitions include “In practice,” “Note that,” “Therefore,” “Meanwhile,” “We therefore propose.”

## Rhetorical moves (common pattern)
1. Context statement with citations.
2. Specific problem/gap statement.
3. Consequence or limitation of current practice.
4. Proposed method or contribution.
5. Brief outline of document structure (“We describe… in §2; results in §3…”).

## Lexicon and diction
- Technical, precise, and domain‑specific; minimal adjectives.
- Prefers “formulation,” “framework,” “implementation,” “methodology,” “workflow.”
- Uses “objective function,” “constraints,” “inventory,” “planning horizon,” “strata.”
- Avoids sales‑pitch terms (“first‑class,” “optimization‑first,” “solver‑agnostic” unless defined).

## Evidence framing
- States empirical support as “illustrative,” “representative,” or “demonstrative,” not “proof.”
- Separates validation (parity, tests) from scope claims; emphasizes reproducibility.
- Distinguishes method contribution from software engineering hygiene (tests, CI) without boasting.

## Section headings and structure
- Clear, straightforward headings.
- Prefers standard scientific structure (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion).
- Uses explicit section cross‑references (“see §3”).

## Practical style rules for this manuscript
- Replace promotional adjectives with precise technical descriptors.
- Use “we” to describe actions, not to assert value.
- Define terms and acronyms on first use; keep definitions short.
- Keep claims scoped to what is demonstrated in the paper; note what is out of scope.
- When referencing software, emphasize transparency, reproducibility, and inspectability.

## Example rewriting cues
- “WS3 delivers X” → “We implement X” or “WS3 implements X.”
- “first‑class” → “documented,” “explicit,” or “implemented.”
- “solver‑agnostic” → “solver‑flexible” + define in the sentence.
- “proves parity” → “provides a reproducible parity illustration.”
